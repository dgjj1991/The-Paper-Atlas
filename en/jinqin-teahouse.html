Chapter 01

What Is This Place?

Jinqin Old Teahouse (金琴老茶馆) opened on September 8, 2024 in the Fuqin neighbourhood of Chengdu's Jinniu District. From the street, it looks like something that's been here forever: water-bucket-thick wooden pillars, grey brick, black roof tiles, a half-open courtyard letting in the sky.

Locals call it "Chengdu's Guanyin Pavilion" — a reference to the legendary century-old teahouse in Pengzhen. But Jinqin has a twist: the teahouse is new; the building is genuinely ancient. The structure is a Qing Dynasty wooden mansion, built in the 1740s in Xichong County, Nanchong — roughly 300 years old — relocated to Chengdu piece by piece to save it from demolition when a highway was built through its original site.

It's run jointly by the Chengdu Peking Opera Troupe and the local Fuqin Street Office. Order a bowl of tea. The Peking opera is free.

Chapter 02

The Story

Before Jinqin, this corner of Fuqin held a decrepit open-air teahouse. In early 2024, the Fuqin Street Office and the Chengdu Peking Opera Troupe invested over ¥2 million to source and reconstruct a genuine piece of old China. They found a Qing Dynasty wooden farmhouse facing demolition for a highway in Nanchong and relocated it using traditional mortise-and-tenon joinery (榫卯).

The floor you walk on is "thousand-foot mud" (千脚泥): the patina of a century of footsteps. The iron stove in the corner burns honeycomb coal and heats copper kettles. You're sitting in the middle of a living rescue operation.

Key moments

  • ~1740s (Qing Dynasty) — Original mansion built in Xichong County, Nanchong.
  • 2023 — Highway construction threatens demolition; Chengdu relocation begins.
  • Sep 8, 2024 — Jinqin Old Teahouse officially opens.
  • Ongoing — Regular Peking opera performances Tue–Fri at 15:00.

Chapter 03

The Space

The teahouse covers roughly 500–600 square metres, divided into front and rear halls by a traditional Sichuan tianjing (天井) courtyard that pulls sky, light, and weather inside.

The Courtyard

Tianjing (天井)

Sunlight pours through translucent roof tiles. On rainy days, water drops in strings from the eaves. Pure Chengdu atmosphere.

The Heart of the Place

The Iron Stove

An old tiger stove (老虎灶) burning honeycomb coal with three large copper kettles bubbling away.

The Floor

Thousand-Foot Mud

Uneven earth floor polished to a subtle sheen by a century of foot traffic.

The Seating

Bamboo Chairs

Traditional woven-bamboo reclining chairs and square Eight Immortals tables with endless boiling water.

Chapter 04

What's On

  • Peking opera (京剧) — Fixed schedule: Tuesday to Friday, 15:00. Performers from Chengdu Peking Opera Research Institute.
  • Sichuan opera (川剧) — Face-changing (变脸), fire-spitting, and clapper storytelling.
  • Amateur opera fans (票友) — Regulars taking the stage in a warm community dialogue.

Chapter 07

Insider Tips

What locals know

  • 01
    Arrive by 14:30 for the 15:00 opera Front-row seats go fast. Settle in with a bowl of tea before the music starts.
  • 02
    Try the "strange-flavour cold noodles" (怪味凉面) ¥10 bowl with sour, sweet, spicy, and sesame numbing goodness.
  • 03
    Rainy days are best Courtyard raindrops on ancient tiles make for an unforgettable, cinematic teahouse experience.
