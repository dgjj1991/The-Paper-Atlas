Chapter 01

What Is This Place?

Youxing Bookstore (有杏书店) is an independent bookstore opened on August 19, 2023, by Zhang Feng (张丰) — journalist, columnist, and author of The Details of Chengdu. The name "有杏" (Youxing) carries a deliberate double meaning: the apricot tree (杏) under which three booklovers once made a pact, and the Mandarin phrase "三生有幸" (sānshēng yǒu xìng) — the fortune of three lifetimes.

Zhang didn't set out to run a shop. He wanted to build a medium — a place that sells ideas (books), produces ideas (events), and gathers the kind of people who care about both. Two years in, while other Chengdu independent bookshops have quietly shuttered, Youxing is still standing.

Chapter 02

The Story

Zhang Feng was born in 1978 in Henan Province, studied Chinese literature at Beijing Normal University, and spent years writing for China Newsweek and New Weekly. He's a Chengdu transplant by choice, and The Details of Chengdu is his love letter to the city.

The bookstore opened inside the decommissioned cargo dock of a 1970s state warehouse, now part of the 107 Creative Park (萌想星球107文创园) in the Erxianqiao neighborhood. The district has become one of Chengdu's densest concentrations of converted industrial space: former factories remade into galleries, record shops, bars, and — now — this bookstore.

The survival story is unusually honest. In June 2024, Zhang returned from a visiting fellowship at Columbia University to find the bookstore's account holding just ¥1,000. He immediately launched a series of paid "Columbia dispatch" lectures at ¥39 per ticket. Nearly 500 people signed up across three sessions. Payroll was covered. The paid-event model has been the bookstore's backbone ever since.

Key moments

  • Aug 2023 — Youxing opens at 107 Creative Park. Authors Xiao Yi, Xiao Yi, and Li Hao join the opening day talks.
  • Jun 2024 — Zhang returns from Columbia with ¥1,000 in the account. Launches emergency paid lecture series. The community responds: nearly 500 tickets across three sessions.
  • 2024 (full year) — Legal scholar Lao Dongyan draws 570 ticket buyers (337 in person). Historian Wang Di, education researcher Lin Xiaoying, and economic historian Adam Tooze all speak.
  • Nov 2024 — "Youxing Classmates" annual pass launches. ¥365/year, all events included. About 300 members by 2025.
  • Aug 2025 — Two years old. Still open while peers close.
  • Oct 2025 — Closure rumours circulate. The reading community rallies. Youxing stays.

Chapter 03

The Space

Zhang describes Youxing as a "three-bedroom home" (三室一厅): two reading rooms at the centre, a coffee bar, a multipurpose event space, and — through a back door most visitors never notice — a courtyard restaurant. The space layers quiet on top of loud, private on top of public.

Room 1 & 2

The Reading Rooms

History, literature, social science. Many titles carry author signatures — Xiao Yi, Yang Zao, He Yujia, Ximenmei. Zhang's personal annotated collection occupies a dedicated shelf: read-only, not for sale, margins filled with his handwriting.

The Kitchen

Youxin Coffee

Every cup comes in a sleeve printed with Sanxingdui (三星堆) artifact motifs and a fortune card you pull from a jar. Bamboo chairs outside face the brick courtyard. Americano: ¥15 — the cheapest good seat in the building.

The Living Room

Event Space

Hidden behind a wooden door flush with the bookshelves. At least three events per week — lectures, readings, book launches. Step through and the reading room's quiet gives way to someone talking about law, history, AI, or education.

Out Front

The Brick Stage

A tiered outdoor amphitheater — the original warehouse loading dock — holding 200+ people. Industrial red brick, geometric shadows, and the best photographic potential on the block. Best light: after 15:00.

Chapter 04

What's On

The event calendar runs through the WeChat public account "城市的地得" (literally: the city's grammatical particles). Check it before your visit — popular tickets sell out.

Past speakers have included legal scholar Lao Dongyan (劳东燕), historian Wang Di (王笛), education researcher Lin Xiaoying (林小英), economic historian Adam Tooze, and journalist Fang Kecheng (方可成).

  • Thought salons — the "思想快闪" (Thought Flash) and "明亮的对话" (Bright Conversations) series
  • Book launches with author Q&A and signed copies available
  • Workshops — flower-petal bookmark printing, holiday crafts, board games, online puzzle evenings
  • Large-scale lectures in the outdoor brick amphitheater
  • Independent bookstore markets — "街上的盐" (Salt on the Street)

Chapter 05

What It Costs

Entry is free. A single coffee earns you an afternoon. Events are the bookstore's financial spine — buying a ticket is the most direct way to keep this kind of place alive.

Americano (Youxin Coffee) ¥15+ Cheapest drink; earns you a bamboo chair all afternoon
Latte / Pour-over ¥20–35 With Sanxingdui cup sleeve + fortune card
Standard talk / lecture ¥29 / session Online or in-person; original "Columbia Dispatch" pricing
Premium scholar lecture ¥39–80+ Larger events with well-known speakers; book in advance
Youxing Classmates Annual Pass ¥365 / year ≈ ¥1/day · All events free · ~300 members as of 2025
Buhou Garden Restaurant ~¥80–150 / person Connected via back door · Courtyard dining · Fusion dishes

Is the annual pass worth it? If you're staying in Chengdu for a week or more and enjoy intellectual conversation — absolutely. For a single visit, pay per event. Either way, the ticket is the support.

Chapter 07

Insider Tips

What the menu doesn't tell you — and what most visitors miss.

What locals know

  • 01
    The ¥15 move Order the Americano. It's the cheapest thing on the menu and earns you an entire afternoon at the bamboo chairs outside. Nobody will rush you. This is Chengdu — people are not in a hurry here.
  • 02
    Don't offer to buy Zhang's books His personal annotated collection is on the shelves labelled 只阅不售 (read only, not for sale). The handwritten notes in the margins are the real attraction. Ask to read one — but don't put a price on it.
  • 03
    Keep the cup sleeve The sleeve on your coffee cup features motifs from Sanxingdui (三星堆) — the mysterious Bronze Age civilization excavated 40km outside Chengdu. It's a considered design choice, not just decoration.
  • 04
    Evening is the real thing The daytime bookstore is pleasant. But events after 19:00 are where Chengdu's literary and intellectual crowd actually gathers. Check WeChat public account "城市的地得" before your trip and buy tickets in advance.
  • 05
    The back door to lunch From the event room, a door leads directly into Buhou Garden Restaurant (布后·花园餐厅) — a courtyard restaurant connected to the bookstore.
  • 06
    Best light for photos The brick steps out front catch warm afternoon light after 15:00. The geometric shadows of the industrial arches make every shot easy.
