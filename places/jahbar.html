Chapter 01

What Is This Place?

JahBar (家吧) is a live music bar on the Nan River in Chengdu's Wuhou District, founded in 2007 by a musician who went by "Lao Li" — a Sichuan northerner who played heavy guitar, fronted bands, and built reggae, ska-punk, and surf groups before deciding Chengdu needed a place like this.

The name is a pun: "家" means home, and "Jah" is Rastafarian for God (as in Reggae). The red, yellow, and green flag above the door is the logo. The philosophy is written on the wall: "For everyone who left their hometown — a place to feel at home in Chengdu."

JahBar is not a professional live house. It doesn't have the lighting rigs, the sound system, or the separation between performer and crowd. What it has instead is something harder to find: no cover charge on weeknights, all ticket revenue from shows goes directly to the band, and a stage open to anyone who wants to play. In Chengdu's increasingly commercial music scene, that's rare enough to matter.

Chapter 02

The Story

Lao Li opened JahBar in 2007 on Hongmen Street by the old Nanmen Bridge — the same spot it still occupies today, facing the Wan Li cruise boat on the Nan River. He was a musician first: rhythm guitarist, vocalist, founder of multiple bands across genres. The bar was never supposed to become an institution. It just kept going.

The management team today includes founder Zhu Jie, and managers Yanzi and Tutu. The rules haven't changed: the bar takes nothing from ticket sales. All money goes to the performers. Free shows are free because the bar absorbs the cost. Musicians get drinks sent to the stage as encouragement even when no one's paying to watch.

JahBar has also opened a second location in Hainan, Sanya — Houhai Village in Haitang Bay — carrying the surf, skateboard, and reggae culture to the coast.

Key moments

  • 2007 — Lao Li opens JahBar on Hongmen Street. The red-yellow-green flag goes up.
  • 2017 — Ten years in. JahBar is established as the de facto gathering place for Chengdu's independent music scene.
  • 2019 — Twelfth anniversary (Apr 25–27). Bands JahWahZoo, Jie Wa, and others perform. The "no ticket fees" policy is reaffirmed publicly.
  • 2024+ — Seventeen years and running. Sanya branch open. The Hongmen Street original still going nightly.

Chapter 03

The Space

JahBar is small. The interior is a square room of a few dozen square metres: bar counter along the walls, booths and stools arranged around a central stage, red LED strips lighting everything from below. The ceiling is hung with the tri-colour flag and band photographs. Guitars and African drums surround the stage on all sides.

Inside

Centre Stage

There is almost no distance between performer and audience. When the band plays, you are close enough to feel the guitar. The red-lit room holds maybe 60 people comfortably; on a packed Saturday it holds more.

Outside

Nan River Terrace

4–5 tables outside the door, facing the Nan River. A foosball table. Simple speakers. You can hear the river mixing with the music from inside. Strangers share tables. One beer and you know everyone.

The Vibe

Rough, Warm, Real

Low-key decor, walls covered in photos of musicians and regulars. Service is semi-self-serve: pick your drink, settle your tab at the counter. No one will rush you to the bar or to the door.

What's On the Walls

17 Years of Nights

Photographs of every band that ever played here — local, visiting, professional, amateur. It's an accidental archive of Chengdu's underground music scene from 2007 to now.

Chapter 04

What's On

Check WeChat public account "JahbarMessage" before you go — it lists upcoming shows, ticketed events, and open stage nights. Don't show up blind on a Saturday expecting free entry.

  • Nightly open jam — Every night without a ticketed show, musicians take the stage and improvise. Punk, hip-hop, jazz, reggae, folk — whoever shows up. Free entry.
  • Weekend headline shows — 5–6 per month, concentrated on Friday and Saturday nights. Past acts include Beijing punk band Diu Laika, Dutch reggae group Neuroot, Russian reggae singer Steppa Style, and Chengdu's own JahWahZoo.
  • Open Stage — Anyone can play. As long as no other act is booked, the stage is yours. If your show is free, the bar sends drinks to the band.
  • Table football + outdoor sessions — The river terrace is its own kind of entertainment on warm nights.

"It's not about the sound system. It's about the zero distance between you and whoever's playing."

— A regular at JahBar, 2024

Chapter 05

What It Costs

JahBar is cheap. The drinks are priced for musicians, not tourists. The economics are deliberate — if the bar were expensive, the crowd would change.

Hot mulled wine (winter) seasonal Special winter menu — warm spiced wine and heated Huadiao rice wine

Weekend headline show ¥12–50+ 100% of ticket revenue goes to the performing band
Average total spend ~¥40–62 Per person, including drinks across an evening

The honest note on sound: JahBar's audio setup is basic. This is a bar, not a concert hall. If you're there for the music, come for the proximity and the atmosphere — not the fidelity.

Chapter 07

Insider Tips

Six things to know before your first night here.

What regulars know

  • 01
    Check JahbarMessage before you go The WeChat public account "JahbarMessage" is the only reliable source for that night's programme. A free jam night and a ticketed show are completely different experiences. Know which you're walking into.
  • 02
    Arrive before 20:30 on weekends The space is small. On Friday and Saturday headline nights, it fills quickly. If you want a seat facing the stage rather than a view of someone's back, arrive early and order immediately — your table is held by your drink.
  • 03
    Start outside, move inside The Nan River terrace is the right place to start an evening — a beer, the sound of water, the city coming alive. Move inside when the music starts properly. The transition is part of the JahBar rhythm.
  • 04
    The bar is semi-self-serve — don't wait to be waited on Walk to the counter, order, pay. There's no table service in the conventional sense. This isn't neglect — it's the culture. The bar is a place where everyone's on equal footing, including the staff.
  • 05
    If you play an instrument, bring it The open stage is genuinely open. If there's no ticketed act on, the stage is available. Several visiting musicians have ended up playing JahBar on a whim. The owner will send you a drink either way.
  • 06
    Book your DiDi before midnight The bar runs until 03:00. After midnight, DiDi availability in this part of Wuhou drops. Arrange your ride home before you lose track of time — which you will.
